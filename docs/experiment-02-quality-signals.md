# Experiment 02 — quality signals: GPT-2 surprisal, char bigrams, POS statistics

Question: the plugin measures momentum well and junk lexically; it does not
measure writing **quality**. Which candidate signal is worth gating rewards
on — GPT-2 surprisal, a character-bigram micro-LM, or POS-tag statistics?

This replaces experiment 01's follow-up probes (`probe*.py`, deleted): those
regressed pooled GPT-2 hidden states against 39 LLM-judge labels with leaky
LOO CV — n too small to mean anything and aimed at the wrong target (judge
salience, not the deployment question "do not reward junk").

## Method

Label-free, deployment-shaped. Corpus (`experiments/corpus.py`): 600-char
windows of the *typed stream* (matching `ContentWindow`) from four sources —
the primary user's real sessions (local only; this repo is public), Gutenberg
fiction, Brown government/learned ("competent non-creative"), and five seeded
junk classes modeling how a human games a typing meter: repeated tokens
("thing thing thing"), keyboard mash ("sdlfkja"), stall filler ("uuuh umm"),
stopword streams, and retyped sentence loops.

Detectors are calibrated on reference prose only (median/MAD or percentiles);
junk labels are used solely for evaluation. Metric: AUC junk-vs-real.

## Results

**GPT-2 124M surprisal band** (distance of mean bits/token from a
fiction-calibrated band; 302 ms/window on this laptop's CPU):

| junk class | GPT-2 band AUC | lexical-gate AUC | gate catch rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| repeat_token | 1.000 | 1.000 | 60/60 |
| keyboard_mash | **1.000** | **0.098** | **0/60** |
| stall_filler | 0.862 | 1.000 | 60/60 |
| stopword_stream | 0.781 | 0.994 | 60/60 |
| loop_sentence | 1.000 | 0.994 | 60/60 |

Two findings. First: **the shipped lexical gate had exactly one hole,
keyboard mash** — mash has *high* char entropy and *zero* repetition, so
every diversity-based facet reads it as healthy prose. Second: GPT-2 and the
lexical facets are complementary — junk is bimodal in surprisal (repetition
collapses to 0.35 bits/token, mash explodes to 8.9; prose bands at ~5.7),
and each detector is strong exactly where the other is weak.

Caveat: raw typed streams (typos, stream-of-consciousness) run hotter than
published fiction (user mean 7.5 vs fiction 5.6 bits/token), so a
fiction-calibrated band flags 11% of real windows at the tight threshold.
Live use would need per-user calibration. Combined with the 302 ms CPU cost,
GPT-2 is a good **offline auditor**, not a runtime gate.

**Char-bigram micro-LM** (27×27 table, Brown-trained, ~3 KB, microseconds):

| source | bits/char |
|---|---|
| real windows (user + fiction) | mean 3.39, p99 3.70 |
| keyboard_mash | 7.37 — AUC **1.000** |
| stall_filler | 5.30 — AUC **1.000** |

The mash hole closes with a lookup table. It also catches uuuh-filler (a
second layer over the token-based facet) and correctly does NOT flag
stopword streams or loops (English-like bigrams — the repetition/entropy
facets own those; every junk class needs one good detector, not every
detector catching everything). **Shipped**: `bigram_bpc` facet in
`content.cpp` (table: `bigram_en.h`, generated by `export_bigram.py`),
gate threshold 4.20 bits/char — clears every observed real window with
margin. Verified: a synthetic 100%-mash session replayed through the real
engine stays 100% gated, zero rewards.

**POS-tag statistics** (NLTK averaged-perceptron tagger, universal tagset):

- Junk robustness: KL divergence of a window's POS distribution from a
  fiction reference: AUC **1.000 on all five junk classes**.
- Creative signature: logistic regression on 16 POS features separates
  Gutenberg fiction from Brown government/learned at **0.956 AUC** under
  grouped CV (no source document shared between train and test). The
  signature: shorter words, more pronouns, higher type-token ratio, higher
  POS-bigram entropy; fewer numbers and adjectives.
- The user's own windows score 0.72 mean on that creative axis (sane:
  stream-of-consciousness drafting reads creative-side, below polished
  fiction).

## Conclusions

1. **Shipped now:** the char-bigram facet. Junk coverage is complete —
   every class ≥0.994 AUC by at least one gate facet, at zero runtime cost.
2. **POS statistics are the promising quality axis** beyond junk: portable
   (perceptron weights export to flat C++ tables), strong on the
   creative/non-creative axis, and fully redundant junk coverage as a side
   effect. Next step if pursued: distinguish *good* creative windows from
   *weak* ones — needs a harder label than genre (the judge-vs-outcome
   question experiment 01 left open).
3. **GPT-2 is the lab instrument**, not the plugin: use it offline to audit
   sessions and validate cheaper facets, not to gate rewards in real time.
4. Honest limits: junk classes are synthetic (adversarial-human junk may
   differ); real-user n is one writer, 10 windows; "creative vs government
   prose" is a genre axis, not a quality axis.

## Addendum — modern tiny-model sweep (experiment 04)

The deleted probe2.py "2026 sub-1GB model sweep" was methodology noise
(leaky LOO on 39 judge labels). Re-run with this experiment's protocol
(`model_sweep.py`): per model, the junk surprisal band + register separation
(fiction / legal-academic / user-rambling; mid-layer mean-pooled hidden
states, nearest centroid, leave-one-source-out CV, fixed mid-layer rule —
no layer selection anywhere).

| model | ms/win (CPU) | mash | loop | repeat | filler | stopword | register acc |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-2 124M (2019) | 347 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.882 | 0.809 | **0.953** |
| SmolLM2-135M (2024) | 397 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.977 | 0.895 | 0.823 | 0.837 |
| Qwen3-0.6B-Base (2025) | 1780 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | **0.509** | 0.588 | 0.874 |

Findings:

1. **No modern tiny model beats GPT-2 on either task.** GPT-2's mid-layer
   states separate the three registers at 0.953 (fiction 0.99 / legal 0.90 /
   rambling 1.00) — best of the three, at 5× less compute than Qwen3.
2. **Stronger LMs are WORSE junk-band detectors.** Qwen3-0.6B collapses on
   stall filler (0.51 ≈ chance) and stopword streams (0.59): a better model
   finds degenerate-but-English text about as predictable as prose, so its
   surprisal band can't separate them. The band signal *depends on* the
   model being mediocre. (Filler/stopwords are caught lexically anyway —
   this matters only if the band were the sole gate.)
3. Caveats: fixed mid-layer + nearest centroid is deliberately selection-free
   — a per-model tuned layer/probe could reorder the ranking, but that
   tuning is exactly what leaked last time; the honest comparison says GPT-2
   is not the bottleneck. Register n = 8 sources, one writer's rambling.

Implication for the register gate (issue #11): the LM rung is viable and
GPT-2-sized — but try the POS/n-gram rungs first; they're free.
